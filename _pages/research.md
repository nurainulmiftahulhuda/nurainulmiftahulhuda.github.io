## Research Overview

My research lies at the intersection of **spatio-temporal statistics**, **time series analysis**, and **environmental risk modelling**. I develop and extend the **Generalized Space-Time Autoregressive (GSTAR)** family of models to better capture dynamic spatial dependence in environmental and public health data across Indonesia, particularly West Kalimantan.

## Current Research Directions

**Dynamic and Data-Driven Spatial Weight Matrices**
Developing spatial weight matrices that adapt over time (rather than fixed contiguity/distance weights), combining multiple environmental layers (temperature, rainfall, PM2.5) through adaptive consensus weighting for more accurate GSTAR-based forecasting.

**Forest Fire and Hotspot Risk Modelling**
Modelling fire radiative power (FRP) and hotspot distribution across Kalimantan using satellite data (NASA FIRMS), combining clustering methods (K-means), GSTAR, and spatial interpolation (Ordinary Kriging) to predict and map fire risk.

**Change of Support in Spatio-Temporal Models**
Investigating how the choice of spatial support (administrative regions vs. regular grids) affects the structural properties and forecasting performance of GSTAR models.

**Environmental and Public Health Risk Prediction**
Applying spatio-temporal statistical methods to environmental hazards (peatland fires, disease outbreaks) and their downstream public health and economic impacts, supported by satellite imagery and IoT sensor data.

## Methods & Tools
GSTAR family models, spatial weight matrix construction, spatial autocorrelation (Moran's Index, LISA), time series intervention analysis, K-means clustering, Ordinary Kriging, R and Python for statistical computing.
